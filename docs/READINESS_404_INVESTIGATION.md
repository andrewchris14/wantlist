## Attribution

**Leading explanation:** an intermediate Worker/forwarding path encountered Cloudflare's Worker-to-Worker fetch restriction. The specific 1042 fingerprint and absence of application network subrequests make this substantially stronger than the earlier generic propagation hypothesis.

**Not proven:** which Worker performed the forbidden fetch, which layer supplied or forwarded the CF-Ray/body, or whether this restriction occurred inside the Codex forwarding path rather than another Cloudflare layer. `Server: envoy` is consistent with an intermediary rewriting/forwarding a response, but does not identify that intermediary. The LAX suffix identifies a Cloudflare edge in the response path, not the source account or the Want List handler's execution. Missing analytics/diagnostics do not mean zero Worker invocations or zero cost.

**Less likely, still unexcluded:** endpoint propagation coinciding with another failure. Immediate timing alone cannot explain away the error-specific evidence. A correct URL and successful control-plane verification do not independently establish that every edge routed to the deployed script.

Do **not** add the compatibility flag to Want List as a speculative fix: the documented restriction concerns the **calling** Worker, and Want List performs no such fetch. Do not bypass the managed proxy, alter TLS policy, change billing, or assume a platform repair is required without contrasting observations.
